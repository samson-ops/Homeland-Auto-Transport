Car Shipping In Ann Arbor, MI: What You Need To Know

Ann Arbor sits in an unusually good spot for auto transport, and it has an unusually busy calendar to match. I-94 runs through town connecting Detroit to Chicago, US-23 heads north toward Flint and south toward Toledo and the Ohio corridor, and Detroit Metro Airport and the entire southeast Michigan freight network are twenty minutes east. Carriers are constantly nearby. What fills those trucks is the University of Michigan: one of the largest and most geographically diverse student bodies in the country, drawing from every state and dozens of nations, generating a substantial wave of vehicle shipments every August and a mirror-image wave every May.

Around the academic rhythm, Ann Arbor's economy adds year-round demand of a distinctive kind. The university health system and the research corridor relocate physicians, researchers, and postdocs from around the world continuously — often people who need a car to arrive without them driving it from wherever they've been. The automotive engineering and mobility sector along the US-23 and I-94 corridors moves professionals in and out on project cycles. And southeast Michigan sends its own steady snowbird flow to Florida and Arizona each October, returning with the spring.

Then there's winter, which is genuinely part of the planning here. Michigan runs salt on the roads from November through March, lake-effect and Alberta clipper systems can idle regional traffic for a day, and the practical realities of loading a car in a Michigan January are worth knowing before your pickup date. What Actually Moves The Price

Corridor position keeps the baseline competitive: I-94 and US-23 carry heavy traffic and the Detroit metro next door deepens the carrier pool considerably. Winter is the other big variable — salt season affects the open-versus-enclosed decision, and a serious snow event can pause regional pickups for a day or two between December and March. Open vs. Enclosed Auto Transport In Ann Arbor

Open car transport is the right call for most southeast Michigan shipments — the same multi-car equipment that has served the auto industry's home region for decades, with the carrier's cargo insurance covering your vehicle bumper to bumper throughout. For a student car making an annual round trip, the enclosed premium buys very little.

Enclosed auto transport earns its 30–50% premium more often in Michigan than in warm-weather states, for one blunt reason: salt. From November through March, Michigan highways run heavy with brine, and a vehicle riding an open trailer collects the same spray as one driving the lane beside it — a car wash for a daily driver, a genuine concern for anything with show paint or fresh undercarriage work. The second case is the collector market, which in the birthplace of the American auto industry runs as deep as anywhere: the summer show and cruise culture here is legendary, and cars headed to auctions, shows, or buyers who paid for perfect sheet metal ship covered as a rule. For everyday vehicles, open transport remains the economical default.

Michigan Realities: U-M Season And Salt Season

Two calendars run Ann Arbor car shipping. The first is the University of Michigan's. Every August, one of the country's most geographically diverse student bodies converges here, and a meaningful share ships a car rather than driving it from California, Texas, New England, or overseas. Every May it reverses. The families who handle it smoothly all do the same three things: book two to three weeks ahead of move-in or move-out; authorize the student (or any adult over 18) to complete the inspection and sign the Bill of Lading, so the shipment never waits on a parent's travel plans; and plan the Ann Arbor handoff somewhere a transporter can genuinely operate. That last point matters more here than people expect — the streets around campus and the older neighborhoods off Main and State are narrow, tree-lined, and, during move-in week, packed with box trucks. Your driver will propose a large lot out toward I-94 or Briarwood instead, and your student drives the last mile.

The second calendar is winter, and it deserves plain talk. From November through March, three things change. Salt gets on everything, which is why enclosed shipping is worth considering for a vehicle you've invested in protecting. Snow events can idle regional pickups for a day or two, so a delivery window beats a delivery date in deep winter. And cold weather quietly kills batteries — the single most common avoidable delay we see in Michigan is a car that won't start when the driver arrives to load it. The preparation is simple: clear all snow and ice from the vehicle so the condition inspection can actually be completed, rinse off accumulated brine if the car's been sitting in it, check the battery the week before pickup, and expect your driver to suggest a cleared main-road meeting spot rather than attempting an unplowed side street. Customers who plan this way have uneventful winter shipments; customers who assume January works like July sometimes don't.

Preparing Your Car For Auto Transport In Ann Arbor

Standard preparation: wash the vehicle for accurate inspection photos (take your own from all four corners), remove personal items, leave about a quarter tank or a 30–50% EV charge, disable the alarm, and confirm battery and tires — vehicles load under their own power.

Southeast Michigan specifics from our dispatch desk: in winter, clear the car completely of snow and ice before the handoff, rinse off road salt if it's been sitting in it, and check the battery the week before pickup. Tell us at booking if the destination is campus-area housing or a narrow tree-lined street in the Old West Side, Burns Park, or Kerrytown, so the meeting spot is planned rather than improvised — typically a large lot near Briarwood or an I-94 interchange. And make sure whoever is receiving the car has keys, a charged phone, and fifteen free minutes; that's genuinely the whole job.
