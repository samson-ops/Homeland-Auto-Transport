Car Shipping In San Antonio, TX: What You Need To Know

San Antonio earned the nickname Military City USA honestly, and it shapes this market more than anything else. Joint Base San Antonio brings together Lackland, Fort Sam Houston, and Randolph into one of the largest military installations in the Department of Defense — Air Force basic training runs through here, the Army's medical education command is here, and tens of thousands of active duty, civilian, and contractor personnel rotate through on PCS cycles every year. Nearly every one of those moves involves at least one privately owned vehicle that has to get somewhere its owner can't drive it. If your shipment is tied to orders, you're in one of the most PCS-experienced markets in the country.

The commercial geography is just as favorable. I-35 runs north through Austin, Dallas, and the middle of the continent while heading south toward Laredo and the border trade; I-10 crosses east toward Houston and west toward El Paso and Southern California. Two of the busiest freight corridors in America intersect here, so carrier availability is deep, pickup windows are short, and San Antonio prices competitively even on long lanes. Add the fastest-growing metro corridor in Texas — the I-35 stretch toward New Braunfels and Austin is one of the country's most active relocation markets — and you have a city where trucks are never far away.

What Actually Moves The Price

Corridor convergence anchors the market: I-35 and I-10 intersecting here means carriers pass constantly in four directions, so San Antonio rarely waits for capacity and long-haul lanes price competitively. PCS season is the sharpest seasonal factor — the summer surge from May through August is a nationwide capacity crunch, and a military town feels it directly on every lane. Vehicle size adds the usual $150–$300 for trucks and three-row SUVs over sedans, and trucks are common cargo out of Texas. Access matters at the margins: a Stone Oak driveway is easier than a downtown high-rise or a narrow King William street, and easy access earns better carrier interest. Enclosed transport adds 30–50%, and non-runners carry a modest winch fee.

Your driver calls before pickup and delivery so you always have a working window. Open vs. Enclosed Auto Transport In San Antonio

Open car transport is the right call for most South Texas shipments — the same multi-car equipment that works the I-35 and I-10 corridors daily, with the carrier's cargo insurance covering your vehicle bumper to bumper throughout. For a PCS move, open transport is almost always the sensible choice, and its deeper capacity means faster assignment during the summer crunch.

Enclosed auto transport earns its 30–50% premium in the usual cases: high-value and collector vehicles, lowered or modified cars needing liftgate loading, and anything with fresh paint worth protecting from a week of highway spray. Texas has a deep truck and hot-rod culture, and lifted or heavily modified vehicles sometimes need special handling regardless of trailer type — tell us the modifications when you book. For everyday vehicles and PCS moves, open transport remains the economical default year-round.

How Our San Antonio Car Shipping Process Works

The process is the same for an airman's truck heading to a new duty station or a family sedan heading to Phoenix, and nothing happens without your approval. First, the quote — instant on this page or by phone, all-inclusive with door-to-door service, fuel, and full insurance. Second, carrier assignment. We post your vehicle to the national dispatch boards, match an FMCSA-licensed carrier running your lane, and verify operating authority, safety record, and current cargo insurance before you approve anything — credentials you can confirm yourself in the FMCSA's public database, and we encourage exactly that. Third, pickup and delivery: joint walk-around inspections at both ends, recorded on the Bill of Lading you and the driver sign and keep. That paperwork is your protection, and we walk every first-time shipper through it.

Preparing Your Car For Auto Transport In San Antonio

Standard preparation: wash the vehicle for accurate inspection photos (take your own from all four corners), remove personal items and any toll transponder, leave about a quarter tank or a 30–50% EV charge, disable the alarm, and confirm battery and tires — vehicles load under their own power.

South Texas specifics from our dispatch desk: if your address is downtown, in King William, or in one of the older neighborhoods near the river with narrow streets and mature trees, flag it at booking and your driver will set a meeting spot with real room — typically a large lot along Loop 410, 1604, or an I-10 or I-35 frontage. For base-area pickups, plan the handoff off-base at one of the corridors above. And in summer, expect morning loadings; South Texas afternoons are hard on drivers and equipment, and a 105-degree steel deck is nobody's friend.
